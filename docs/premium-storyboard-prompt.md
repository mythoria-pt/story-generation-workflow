# How Mythoria chooses a picture-book spread

Mythoria chooses a layout for each story moment using the audience the family already selected. The generator first decides what is worth saying aloud, then finds room for those words. For new editions, the illustration brief adapts the family's chosen style into a picture-book medium. Palette, shapes and mark-making can express watercolour, collage or a cinematic style. Character photographs guide likeness. Toddler books favour a few clearly separated shapes. Preschool books leave room for small details children may discover on a second reading.

## The prompt used by generation

The premium worker passes the output of [buildPremiumStoryboardPrompt](../src/services/premium-book-storyboard.ts) to the configured text model before it requests illustrations. The [storyboard schema](../src/services/premium-book-contract.ts) validates its response. The saved storyboard fixes the page plan, language, layout and canonical words for subsequent stages and retries. New editions paint the narration into the illustration. An independent visual review transcribes each trimmed page and compares the result with the saved words. Older editions keep their typeset boxes and overflow checks.

The prompt reserves page 33 of a new book for the publisher’s localized closing invitation and ends the narrative on page 32. The generator supplies the invitation text and link from the book’s locale. Older runs that froze a 32-page plan use the corresponding 32-page prompt.

The current renderer supports one painting per spread with two possible text placements. In `right_only` the painting continues across the fold and the narrator speaks on the right. In `both_pages` each half has a complete spoken thought. Each occupied narrative page has a 110-character limit and the finished lettering must fit within the safe area at a readable size. That limit comes from the Mythoria format. The research below informs editorial choices about focus and shared reading.

The same storyboard call chooses one small recurring object and a light pastel for the opening pages. For Inês, a paper boat on warm cream recalls the folded paper. A leaf on sage could suit a garden story. A new book identifies the spread that shows the object best. The worker uses that illustration as a reference for one small ornament and retains the curated selection as a fallback if needed. The title and dedication carry the same drawing. The copyright and synopsis use the chosen background alone.

## Age, rhythm and attention

For ages 0–2, start with a familiar object or action a child can point to and a line an adult enjoys repeating. A sound can carry an entire spread. Two voices can also justify one brief line on each page. The American Academy of Pediatrics encourages pointing, naming familiar objects, simple rhymes and repetition with babies. Reach Out and Read describes predictable language and rhymes among its toddler book selections. [American Academy of Pediatrics](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/Pages/How-to-Share-Books-with-Your-Baby.aspx) · [Reach Out and Read](https://reachoutandread.org/books/)

For ages 3–6, let a character want something, try something and see what follows. A second line can add a reply or explain why a small action matters. Leave pauses when a child can infer the answer from the illustration. The What Works Clearinghouse recommends conversation and retelling during shared reading with preschoolers. These are recommendations for shared reading that Mythoria adapts editorially to the book. [IES practice guide, recommendation 7](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/ReferenceResources/TO4_summary_rec_7.pdf)

Paired images demand particular care when introducing a new word. In two experiments with children around 3½, children learned fewer new labels when they had to locate the named object across two competing illustrations. A guiding gesture addressed the difficulty. This finding concerns a specific word-learning task. It supports keeping a named object easy to find and leaves room for paired images when the story benefits. [Flack and Horst, _Two sides to every story_](https://doi.org/10.1002/icd.2047)

Both pages are visible together in print. A pause between looking left and reading right works within a spread. A surprise that depends on hiding the outcome belongs after the next page turn. The binding, trim and final text size also influence where an important object or line can sit. Check the resulting individual PDF pages and the physical proof. [Lulu Book Creation Guide](https://assets.lulu.com/media/guides/en/lulu-book-creation-guide.pdf)

## Suggested wording when adding the proposed layouts

This is the proposed addition to the storyboard instructions once the extra compositions have reader, artwork, accessibility and print support. The production prompt accepts only the two implemented placements.

> Use the supplied audience, locale and list of enabled layouts. Make a choice for each spread. First identify the moment the child should notice and the shortest natural passage that preserves its meaning. Decide whether the pictures show one continuous moment, two successive moments, or one image that gives a longer passage room to breathe.
>
> For one moment, use one painting. Choose text on the right for a pause or a line that follows looking. Use text on both pages for an exchange or two connected thoughts. If paired illustrations are enabled, choose them when seeing two distinct moments together adds meaning and each scene is easy to follow. If a full illustration facing a text page is enabled, choose it when the spoken passage genuinely needs the space and one image can carry the scene. A family preference can break a tie between equally readable layouts.
>
> Review the spreads in sequence and in the book's language. Preserve the character, the story and the normal type size. Where space is tight, read the line aloud, edit it or move a complete thought to another spread. Use only enabled layouts. If explaining your choice to a family, give one short reason in their language that refers to this story moment.

Keep the default automatic. If more compositions become available, offer one optional book-wide preference with thumbnail examples. In European Portuguese the choices could read **A Mythoria escolhe**, **Mais espaço para as ilustrações** and **Um pouco mais de texto**. Use the existing age selection and keep parents free to proceed without another decision. The preference changes the balance among workable options. The same physical space can hold different amounts of text in different languages.
